Observability isn't limited to software. Teams, organizations, and delivery processes are systems too — and they produce signals that can be measured, analyzed, and acted upon. But observing human systems requires a fundamentally different approach than observing technical ones.

The guidance in this section is built on a core principle: **individuals must feel and be safe while being observed.** Telemetry about human systems must be anonymized, aggregated, and never used for individual evaluation or compensation decisions. Misusing this data erodes trust and produces the opposite of the intended outcome.

## What's Here

- **[The Ethics of Observing Human Systems](./measurement-ethics/)** — An opinionated specification (RFC 2119) for how to measure teams without destroying trust. The foundational reading for everything else in this section.
- **[Delivery Metrics](./delivery-metrics/)** — DORA metrics, engineering metrics, DevEx, and platform health indicators.
- **[Satisfaction & Well-Being](./satisfaction/)** — Qualitative measures of team health, joy, and culture.
- **[Engineering Practices](./engineering-practices/)** — Reference material on practices that delivery metrics are built upon.

## The Approach

These metrics should be observed as a set — focusing on one inherently diminishes the value of others. They are signals, not scorecards. Teams use them for self-evaluation and continuous improvement. Leadership uses them to understand organizational health, never to rank or punish.

Read [The Ethics of Observing Human Systems](./measurement-ethics/) first.
